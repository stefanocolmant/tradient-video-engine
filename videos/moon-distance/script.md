You'd think the Moon is close. It isn't.

It sits about three hundred eighty-four thousand kilometers away. That gap could hold thirty Earths, side by side.

Even light needs more than a second to cross it.

The Apollo 11 crew needed about three days just to get there.

And it's drifting. Every year, the Moon slips almost four centimeters farther away. About as fast as your fingernails grow.

So the Moon you'll see tonight is a little farther than the one astronauts walked on.
